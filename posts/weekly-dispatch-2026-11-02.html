This week's edition is written for studio owners, and it opens with a production truth every studio owner learns sooner or later: your room is part of your gear.

This week's feature: treatment before new gear

You can buy a beautiful microphone, a clean preamp, and good monitors — and still get recordings that sound boxy, muddy, or harsh. That's usually not the gear. It's the sound bouncing off bare walls, a low ceiling, and hard floors, arriving at the mic a fraction of a second after the real voice and smearing it. No plugin fully undoes that, because the reflection is baked into the recording.

Treatment isn't about making a room silent — it's about controlling reflections. A treated room gives you a clean, honest recording to work with. An untreated room gives you a recording plus the room, whether you wanted it or not, on every single take.

Try this: clap once in the middle of your room and listen. If you hear a fluttery, metallic ring after the clap, that's reflections talking. Now clap again in a closet full of clothes, or with a heavy blanket hung on the wall behind you. The clap gets shorter, drier, and clearer. That's what even basic treatment does: it shortens the reflections so the direct sound — the voice, the instrument — comes through first and cleanest. Start where reflections hit first: the walls to the left and right of the performer, and the wall behind them.

The pro move: treat the first reflection points before you upgrade a microphone. A mid-priced mic in a treated room will beat an expensive mic in a bare, echoey room on almost every session. And keep a little life in the room — you want controlled, not dead. A room with no reflections at all can sound flat and tiring; the goal is a room that helps the performance, not one that swallows it.

When you want to hear the difference a properly treated room makes before you invest in your own, the Lab has you covered. Sessions are $65/hour in our soundproof, treated room — come record one song in it, then take the same setup home and compare. You'll hear the room, or rather, you won't.
